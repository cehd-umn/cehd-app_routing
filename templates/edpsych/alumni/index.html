“I conduct research with young children and their families to explore language development and interventions within naturally occurring routines.”

Anne Larson, Ph.D '16
Assistant professor, Utah State University

Anne researches early language development and studies sociocultural and environmental factors that affect child-caregiver interactions.

She also teaches course in the birth-to-5 program to prepare early childhood special educators for work with children who have disabilities, as well as the parents and caregivers who who help support those children.

Where are they now

Our alumni find jobs. In fact, 100% of our counseling and student personnel psychology and school psychology students found jobs in 2015. Dedicated to improving the way people learn and grouw, our graduates work in schools and communities and conduct cutting edge research in educational psychology.

Below are just a few examples of our graduates' careers.

Counseling and student personnel

  • Psychology
  • Licensed K-12 school counselor
  • College and student personnel counselor
  • Mental health professional in a community agency

Psychological foundations of education

  • Governmental Agencies
  • Health care organization evaluator/researcher
  • Human and social service organizations
  • Research scientists at universities and corporations
  • Teaching and research at colleges and universities (Ph.D. only)
  • Other educational settings (e.g., K-12 school research offices)

Quantitative methods in education

  • Public school systems
  • Private industry
  • Research and evaluation centers
  • State departments of instruction
  • Teaching and research at colleges and universities (Ph.D. only)
  • Test publishing firms

More info on where quantitative methods in education graduates found jobs.

School psychology

  • M.A./Specialist: Work in public and private schools, preschools, and state depar of education.
  • Ph.D.: Work in universities (as faculty), mental health agencies, research centers and think tanks, clinical settings, state departments of education, and independent practices.

Special education

  • B.S./ABS licensure: Teach individuals in special education from birth to age 21
  • M.Ed./licensure: Teach individuals in special education from birth to age 21
  • M.A./Ph.D.: Conduct research, teach in universities, and work in other educational settings in special education.
  • State departments of instruction
  • Teaching and research at colleges and universities (Ph.D. only)
  • Test publishing firms
