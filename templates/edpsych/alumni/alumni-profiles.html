Counseling and student personnel psychology

Abby WhalenSchool counselor

Abby Whalen initially pursued a degree in chemistry, but wasn't sure what she wanted to do after graduation. She took two years to do volunteer work and during this time found a passion for school counseling. Read more about Abby's story here.

Jeanette VyhanekSchool counselor

Jeanette Vyhanek was a high school principle's assistant before realizing her desire to work more closely with students. This led her to pursue her M.A. in the CSPP program. Read more about her story here.

Marin ThuenSchool counselor

Marin Thuen is currently a school counselor at Olson Middle School in North Minneapolis. In this role she does individual counseling, small group counseling, classroom lessons, crisis counseling, therapy referrals, and serves as a member of several school-wide teams. Read more about how the CSPP program prepared and challenged her for her future career here.

Masha FinnCareer counselor

Masha Finn didn't take one psychology class during her undergraduate career and studied french and francophone studies. A work study position as a career advisor gave her a taste of counseling, ultimatley leading her to pursure her M.A. in the CSPP program. Read more about Masha's story here.

Psychological foundations of education

Lara JessenData scientist

Lara Jessen is a data scientist for Minneapolis Public Schools, where she supports community partners and schools eith data-based decision making and data literacy. Read more about how Lara's experience in the psych foundations program and how she got to where she is now.

Mike MensinkAssociate professor

Mensink graduated with a B.A. in psychology from St. Olaf College in 2002 and after graduation worked a number of odd jobs, including Best Buy salesman and TSA security screener. In 2003 he was hired as a Laboratory Instructor and Manager at Macalester College, and his experiences in that job kindled his interest in pursuing a graduate degree specifically focused on understanding learning. Mensink started pursuing a M.A. degree but eventually switched to the Ph.D. track in 2006 and finished in 2011. Read more about Mike's story here.

Sarah CarlsonAssistant professor

Sarah Carlson received her bachelor’s degree in psychology, where she found an interest in research and found she was interested in applying what she was learning to an educational environment. This led her to look for Education Psychology programs, and she found she was most interested in the University of Minnesota. Carlson ended up completing both her M.A. and Ph.D. in the psychological foundations in education program. Read more about her experience here.

Virginia ClintonAssistant professor

Virginia Clinton began as a classroom teacher in New York City and started becoming curious about how her students were learning. This lead her to read about educational psychology on her own, and promoted her to get her Ph.D. in the topic. Now an Assistant professor at the Univeristy of North Dakota, Clinton both teaches as well as does research. Read more about Clinton and her experience here.

Quantitative methods in education

Alison PhillipsSenior quantitative analyst

Alison Phillips always knew she wanted to be a high school math teacher—until she didn’t. After two high-school teaching positions, Phillips decided to leave that field and puruse her graduate degree in the QME program. Read more about Alison's story here.

Brandon LeBeauAssistant professor

Brandon LeBeau, an alumni of the quantitative methods in education (QME) program is currently Assistant Professor of Educational Measurement and Statistics at the University of Iowa. In this position he teaches applied statistics courses at the undergraduate, masters, and doctoral level. Read more about Brandon and how he got to where he is.

Katherine EdwardsData and research analyst

Katherine Edwards received her bachelor’s in math and psychology with minors in education and statistics. Her psychology major allowed her to study abroad in Denmark where she participated in an educational psychology program and was introduced to their education system. In Denmark and after talking to some teachers, she decided she would be better off doing something else in education other than teaching. Edwards knew she wanted to pursue a graduate degree, leading her to the Department of Educational Psychology's QME program. Read more about Katherine's story here.

Laura ZieglerLecturer

Before coming to U of M, Laura Ziegler had been teaching for 6 years. Now a lecuter at Iowa State University, Ziegler explains how what she learned in graduate school completely changed her approach to teaching. Read more about Laura and her experience in the QME program here.

Okan BulutAssociate professor

Okan Bulut received his bachelor’s degree in elementary education in Turkey. Knowing he wanted to expand his knowledge, Bulut applied for a graduate scholarship and decided to pursue a degree abroad. Impressed by the QME program and faculty, Bulut applied and found himself in Minneapolis a few weeks later. Read more about Okan and his experience here.

Quinton LovePsychometrician

Quinton Love was a middle school math teacher in 2015 and initially pursued Mathematics Education at the University of Minnesota. However, after taking some courses in the Educational Psychology department, knew he had to switch. Read more about Quinton's experience in the QME program and where he is now.
