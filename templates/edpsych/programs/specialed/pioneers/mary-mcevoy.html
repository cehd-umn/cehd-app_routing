Mary McEvoy

Originally from Knoxville, Tennessee, Professor Mary McEvoy received her Bachelor’s degree in Special Education and her Master’s and PhD degrees in Child and Family Studies from the University of Tennessee. She was then a researcher and professor at Vanderbilt University. Her research at Vanderbilt University focused on effective practices for including students with disabilities in early childhood settings. Her service to individuals with disabilities was not limited to young children; she was also a board member for an advocacy program for adults with disabilities. In 1990, Professor McEvoy joined the University of Minnesota’s College of Education and Human Development. During her tenure at the University of Minnesota, Professor McEvoy held positions in research, teaching, and leadership.

Professor McEvoy’s scholarly work focused on early childhood development, specifically effective inclusion for young children with disabilities. Her contributions to the area of early childhood include development of interventions to teach social interaction, as well as prevention and intervention of challenging behavior for young children (McConnell, 2004). In addition, Professor McEvoy and her colleagues developed tools such as the widely used Individual Growth and Development Indicators (IGDIs) for preschool-aged children, which were designed to measure a child’s overall development. She also contributed to the knowledge of how environmental risk and prenatal substance exposure affect developmental trajectories (McConnell, Priest, Davis, & McEvoy, 2002; Carta, Atwater, Greenwood, McConnell, McEvoy, & Williams, 2001).

Professor McEvoy served as the chair of the Department of Educational Psychology, the director of the Center for Early Education and Development (CEED), and held multiple leadership roles in national organizations such as the Division for Early Childhood of the Council for Exceptional Children. She also was involved in state and national politics for the Democratic Farm Labor Party in Minnesota, and worked with policy makers on issues such as social justice.

Until her death in 2002, Professor Mary McEvoy made extensive contributions to early childhood development and early childhood special education. She is known for her leadership, dedication to teaching, and commitment to improving services for people with disabilities. To honor her legacy, the Division of Early Childhood and the University of Minnesota created awards such as the Light a Candle Award, Mary A. McEvoy Award for Public Engagement and Leadership, and the Mary McEvoy Service to the Field Award, for individuals who display exemplary leadership and contribute to the development of young children.
