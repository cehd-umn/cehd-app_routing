Frank Wood

Professor Frank Wood dedicated his career to improving outcomes for children with emotional and behavioral disabilities. His contributions to the field of special education are prolific; and yet, his career humbly began in the public-school classroom. In 1956, Professor Wood worked as a fifth-grade, classroom teacher in the Minneapolis School District. In the classroom, he was drawn to teaching students with special needs; however, he felt his effectiveness as an instructor was limited due to the number of students in the general education classroom. In 1958, he left the general education classroom to become the first public school teacher for students classified as emotionally disturbed. At this time, Professor Wood began a master’s program at the University of Minnesota where he studied mental health under Professor Bruce Barlow and Professor Frank B. Widerson; later, these two men, along with Professor Stan Deno, would encourage him to pursue a PhD. In 1965, Professor Wood was the first University of Minnesota PhD student to graduate with a specialization in emotional and behavioral disabilities. Following graduation, he became a faculty member of the Department of Educational Psychology’s Special Education Program at the University of Minnesota.

His work is primarily based on developing policies, programs, and interventions for students with emotional and behavioral disorders. Professor Wood conducted several studies on assessment, placement procedures and social behaviors of students. He was author and editor of over 120 journal articles, book chapters, and conference proceedings, and he presented over 150 presentations in professional meetings and conferences during his career. At the same time, Professor Wood co-established the Minnesota Educators of the Emotionally Disturbed and the Minnesota Council for Children with Behavioral Disorders (CCBD). In addition to his academic responsibilities, Wood held positions at the national level, including the presidency of CCBD and the governor of both the Council for Exceptional Children (CEC) and CCBD.

Prominent publications:

Wood, F. H. (1994). `Why should I? That's not my problem.' Defensive Thought and Behavior Patterns and Children's Resistance to Adult-Sponsored Socialization. Education & Treatment of Children, 17(3), 302-311.

Wood, F. H. (1991). Cost/Benefit Considerations in Managing the Behavior of Students with Emotional/Behavioral Disorders. Preventing School Failure, 35(2), 17-23.

Wood, F. H. (1973). Negotiation and Justification: An Intervention Model. Exceptional Children, 40(3), 185-190.
