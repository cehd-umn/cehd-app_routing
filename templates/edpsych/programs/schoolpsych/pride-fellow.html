The school psychology program at the University of Minnesota has five Project PRIDE fellowships available to students enrolling in the Master’s/Specialist Certificate track for Fall 2017.

What is Project PRIDE?

Project PRIDE is a fellowship program supported by a training grant from the U.S. Department of Education Office of Special Education Programs. Fellows complete various training activities to develop knowledge and skills to implement and support evidence-based multi-tiered systems of supports (MTSS) in diverse communities, including: data-based decision making, early intervention, prevention, and contemporary models of special education eligibility determination.

Fellowship benefits

This fellowship covers approximately $77,000 of the cost of graduate school over three years including:

  • An annual stipend of $15,000 (years one and two)
  • Health and dental insurance (years one and two)
  • Tuition remission (all three years)
  • $750-1,000 annual allotment for professional development

Requirements

During the program, Project PRIDES fellows must:

  • Complete all school psychology degree requirements.
  • Identify and develop a specialty area through elective courses, thesis, and research apprenticeships.
  • Complete research apprenticeships to develop research skills.
  • Complete fieldwork in high-need schools in diverse, urban communities.
  • Participate in monthly professional development activities.

After completing the program, Project PRIDES fellows must:

  • Apply for state certification and be a Nationally Certified School Psychologist (NCSP) upon graduation.
  • Maintain employment in a school for two years for each year of the fellowship (i.e., six years).
  • Complete the service requirement above within 11 years of graduation. Fellows who fail to meet the requirements above (program completion or employment) are obligated to repay the support received (approximately $77,000 for a three-year fellowship)

How to apply

  • Submit all application materials required for the school psychology program, and
  • In one (single-spaced) page or less, describe the following: (1) interests in MTSS and evidence-based practice, and serving high-need children with or at risk for disabilities, and (2) proposed specialty area(s).

Contact: Amanda Sullivan at asulliva@umn.edu with questions about the fellowship program.
