Sports Management BS

About the program

Program overview

The bachelor of science (B.S.) program in sport management is for students who are interested in working with professional, collegiate, or community sports and athletic organizations. Students will gain knowledge and experience in the many aspects of sport, enabling them to find a career path that will excite and challenge them.

The sport management major has dedicated faculty who are experts in sport, ranging from leadership in sport, to marketing, to policy and administration. Our faculty have experience in both for-profit and not-for-profit organizations, and excel in connecting research and academics to hands-on experiences. Students will have robust experiences both in and outside of the classroom.

Degree competencies

The University of Minnesota sport management program prepares students to learn about the exciting and developing business of sport through multiple outlets:

  • Ethics and law
  • Diversity in sport
  • Sales and fundraising
  • Marketing and business
  • Facilities and event operation

Outside of the classroom, students in the sport management program gain hands-on experience that prepares them to enter the workforce through:

  • Job shadowing and networking
  • Study abroad and national student exchange
  • Research and involvement
  • Practical experience/practicum

Major requirements / course catalog

Core courses

The purpose of the core courses (34 credits) is to provide students with a broad and diverse understanding of the industry. Students will have the opportunity to engage in diverse topics, work in small groups, and hear from industry experts to further their understanding of the industry.

See the University Catalog for information on the current core courses and their descriptions.

Focused electives

The purpose of focus electives (23 credits) is to provide a greater understanding of individual students’ topics of interest that tie into future professional goals. Students can take courses from the approved list of focus elective courses, or choose their own focus elective courses after discussing their course ideas with their departmental adviser. Students may also want to consider a complimentary minor program when deciding on their focus elective courses. Minor courses can also fulfill focus elective requirements with adviser approval. See the University Catalog for information on the current approved focused electives.

Internships and career exploration

What graduates do?

A degree in sport management is applicable in a wide variety of careers. Below are a few highlighted opportunities, but the opportunities stretch far and wide!

Marketing and promotion

Sports marketing professionals are in charge of the promotion of sports events and teams, including broadcasting, advertising, social media, digital platforms, ticket sales, and community relations. They generally love sports, and also understand the history, attitudes, and environment of players and fans.

Salary Estimate: $51K | Estimated 10% job growth over the next 10 years | Top Traits: analytical, creative, excellent communicator

Facilities management and operations

Facilities managers provide a safe, clean, and comfortable learning, working, and living environment for people on a day-to-day basis. They are in charge of overseeing health and safety, cleaning, security and building maintenance.

Salary Estimate: $70K | Estimated 9% job growth over the next 10 years | Top Traits: leadership, business-oriented, strategic

Coach and scout

Coaches teach amateur or professional athletes the skills they need to succeed at their sport. Scouts look for new players and evaluate their skills and likelihood for success at the college, amateur, or professional level. Many coaches are also involved in scouting.

Salary Estimate: $34K | Estimated 13% job growth over the next 10 years | Top Traits: excellent communicator, dedicated, leadership

Practicum internship experience

All sport management students are required to participate in SMGT 3996 Practicum in order to graduate. Practicum is an 8-credit/360 hour (1 credit equals 45 hours) experience at one or two sites that is designed to be completed at the end of the degree. This is similar to a senior internship. In order to start a practicum experience students need to have:

  • The majority (half or more) of their Core Major Courses completed
  • Half (10 credits) of their Focus Elective coursework completed

In order to register for SMGT 3966, it is recommended that students first consult with their departmental advisor in order to ensure they have completed the prerequisites for the practicum experience and understand the practicum requirement. Once they have a qualifying opportunity lined up, students should contact the supervising faculty member to request the practicum manual and fill out the Practicum Learning Agreement form. Students should contact their supervising faculty member once you have secured a practicum experience and discuss when earning credits would work within the academic calendar. Once the supervising faculty member has approved the position, students will receive a permission number to register for SMGT 3996.

During their experience students are required to keep weekly logs and write a final reflection paper. Additionally, the supervising faculty member will have conversations with the student’s site supervisor to discuss the professional experience and progress made throughout the semester.

Practicum can be completed over two semesters or one more intense semester. Coaching experiences are not eligible for practicum. For full details and requirements, students should review the Practicum Learning Agreement form.
