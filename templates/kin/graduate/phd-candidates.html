PhD graduates 2018-2019

Raiza Beltran

belt0013@umn.edu | Curriculum Vitae

Advisor: Elizabeth Lightfoot, PhD

Research interests: Social determinants and impacts of reproductive health; Global reproductive justice; Immigrant and refugee health; Community based participatory research; Global urban poverty

Teaching interests: Social work practice and health; Global/international social work; Diversity/equity/disparities; Research methods; Community based participatory research

Raiza Beltran received her BA in Journalism at the University of Minnesota and her MPH at University of Minnesota School of Public Health in Community Health Promotion. Her research interests focus on understanding the societal and cultural factors influencing the sexual health outcomes of young people living in underserved local and global communities. As a PhD candidate with a minor in epidemiology, Raiza is working on a mixed methods doctoral dissertation and is examining how religious and gender ideology influences contraceptive behavior among unmarried young Filipino women.

Molly Calhoun

bcalho079@umn.edu | Curriculum Vitae

Advisor: Elizabeth Lightfoot, PhD

Research interests: Urban restructuring and the impact on historic residents and communities; Affordable and subsidized housing models; Perception and role of social work in society

Teaching interests:Individual, family, group and community practice; Anti-oppressive social work practice; Social policy; Program development; Research methods

Molly Calhoun received her BA in Psychology from Colorado College and her MSW from the University of Denver. Molly spent over 15 years working in positive youth development programs and community-based practice with children, youth and families in San Francisco, CA, and Denver, CO. Molly’s research interests include the impact of urban restructuring on historically marginalized communities, as well as comprehensive community initiatives that aim to mitigate barriers and promote the well-being of the residents of these communities. Molly is a research assistant for Dr. Jessica Toft in the School of Social Work and Dr. Brittany Lewis at the Center for Urban and Regional Affairs (CURA).

Minhae Cho

bcalho079@umn.edu | Curriculum Vitae

Advisor: Wendy Haight, PhD

Research interests: Child maltreatment; Delinquency; Developmental outcomes of culturally diverse and vulnerable youth; Racial disparities in social service systems

Teaching interests:Child welfare practice; Assessment and engagement with children and families; Social work research methods; Diversity and cultural competence

Minhae Cho received her BA and Master’s degrees in Child Welfare from the Sookmyung Women’s University, South Korea. She completed her MSW at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities. She has six years of professional practice experience in child welfare in Korea. While in this position, she engaged in both direct practice work with children and families and macro practice for policy changes related to child welfare and nation-wide awareness programs for diversity. She also planned and secured a 20-year longitudinal research project to examine developmental outcomes of domestically adopted children in Korea. Based on her practice and academic experiences, Minhae’s research interests are focused on the social, emotional, and relational well-being of culturally diverse and vulnerable children and families involved in multiple social service systems.

Sharyn DeZelar

dezel042@umn.edu | Curriculum Vitae

Advisor: Elizabeth Lightfoot, PhD

Research interests: Direct practice courses, both generalist and clinical: Individuals, families, groups, disabilities; Social welfare history, and History of the social work profession; Social welfare policy; Research methods and applied research courses

Teaching interests:Child welfare practice; Assessment and engagement with children and families; Social work research methods; Diversity and cultural competence.

Sharyn DeZelar received her BSW from the University of Wisconsin-Eau Claire. She worked for several years as a case manager for families with multiple episodes of homeless who were deeply entrenched in poverty and public systems. After receiving her MSW from St. Catherine's University/University of St. Thomas, she began working as a mental health clinician with similar populations. Sharyn’s research interests include children with disabilities and their families, disparities in access to diagnoses, interventions and supportive services of people with disabilities, and the involvement of these families in the child welfare system and other public systems. As a research assistant, Sharyn has worked with child welfare administrative data focused on parents with disabilities and a qualitative study focused on social workers with disabilities. Sharyn has taught several social work courses at both the BSW and MSW levels and is currently working as a research associate on an intervention study, testing the effectiveness of a supportive intervention for parents with disabilities.
