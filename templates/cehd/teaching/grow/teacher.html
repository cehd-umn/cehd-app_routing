MNGOT: M.Ed. and Initial Elementary Teaching License or K-12 ESL Teaching License

Minnesota school districts have a goal to increase the racial and linguistic diversity of our teachers. In 2015, the College of Education and Human Development (CEHD) created a Multiple Pathways to Teaching initiative. Our objective is to diversify the teaching profession by creating new pathways to becoming a fully licensed teacher. We are expanding our Minneapolis Residency Program to include more districts and spreading out the time it takes to complete (from one year to two years). The 36-credit program is called the University of Minnesota Grow Your Own Teachers Program (MNGOT).

Is this program right for you?

The University of Minnesota Grow Your Own Teachers Program is designed for individuals who have an undergraduate degree and are currently employed in a partner district, including:

  • Educational assistants or paraprofessionals
  • Substitute/reserve teachers who hold only a substitute teaching license
  • Other teachers who are teaching on a district waiver, but who do not have another US teaching licenser

Advantages of this program

  • Stay in residence at current employer
  • A teacher community (cohort) learning together for 2 years
  • Face-to-face summer courses and hybrid (online and face-to-face) courses during the academic year
  • Practicing teachers can complete the program while continuing to work
  • University/district agreement to facilitate a leave of absence for student teaching; possibility of paid student teaching if funding allows.
  • Innovative, equity-driven curriculum
  • University coursework concurrent with field-based experiences
  • University supervision in all school placements throughout the program

Program schedule

Year One: Students work full-time in their districts and complete 18 credits.

Year Two: Three options are available.

  • Leave district staff role and become a full-time teacher candidate (with a paid stipend* as a “resident”) and co-teach for the full year; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits
  • Take a leave of absence for 16 weeks of student teaching; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits
  • Be hired as a teacher of record on a Tier I or Tier II license; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits

Tuition and financial aid information

We are able to offer a reduced rate of $600 per credit. The 36-credit program will cost a total of $21,600; this rate will save participants nearly $3000. In addition, working is allowable during at least the first year of the program, depending on individual student teaching scenarios and district employment opportunities.

Undergraduate course requirements*

K-6 Elementary License

You must have completed courses in at least three of the five following areas before we can recommend you for a teaching license. A grade of C- or above is required. Up to two missing prerequisite courses may be completed during the program.

  • Literature
  • Introductory science (including biology, physics, chemistry, or astronomy)
  • College-level algebra
  • Social Science (such as history, geography, anthropology, or political science)
  • Introductory or General Psychology

K-12 ESL (English as a Second Language) License

  • Introductory or General Psychology (a grade of C- or above is required)
  • One year of college-level language study other than your primary language or significant equivalent coursework in another language

*Non-native speakers of English must meet additional University language requirements.

Steps for Application

To begin the application process, fill out a the MNGOT inquiry form.

Our advisor, Jade Beauclair, will follow up via email requesting unofficial transcripts.

Following the review for prerequisite courses, we will send a document with complete application instructions. The application will require a resume with relevant experience, letters of recommendation, and an interview.
