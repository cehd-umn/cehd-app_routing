MNGOT: District partnerships

Expanding our partnerships

Minnesota school districts have a goal to increase the racial and linguistic diversity of our teachers. In 2015, the College of Education and Human Development (CEHD) created a Multiple Pathways to Teaching initiative. Our objective is to diversify the teaching profession by creating new pathways to becoming a fully licensed teacher. To this end, we have several grow-your-own partnerships with school districts, including the Minneapolis Residency Program (MRP) with the Minneapolis Public Schools. This recruitment strategy offers a pathway to teaching for non-licensed staff who have earned a bachelor’s degree. The students in the program are both full time district staff and full time graduate students. Our first three MRP cohorts of approximately 25 candidates each year have had more than 60% people of color and more than 50% bilingual people.

We are expanding the partnership to include more district partners and are moving from a one-year model to a two-year model. The 36-credit elementary licensure program will be renamed the University of Minnesota Grow Your Own Teachers Program (MNGOT). In addition, we are seeking state approval to K-12 ESL, 5-8 mathematics, 5-8 science, and 9-12 life science, chemistry, and physics to the program. We are seeking partners to collaborate with us in the preparation of their non-licensed staff to be become teachers.. In the partnership, the school district and the university contribute staff and resources to the implementation of the program.

What are the costs and benefits?

The program is an investment with costs to the student (tuition), district (teacher payment stipend and benefits), and the university (subsidized programming with lower tuition rate). The benefit for the student is earning an initial teaching license and a masters in education, the ability to retain one’s employment, and a deep induction process into the district. The benefits for the district far outweigh the costs: employers are able to recruit from within the district and grow talent, increase the number of teachers of color and bilingual teachers, and contribute to the preparation of future teachers and their longevity in the field. The benefit to the university is the opportunity to increase the number of teachers of color in the state, to be responsive to our district partners, and to document the most effective way to prepare teachers.

How can a district participate?

Join our 2018-2020 elementary cohort. Your district may apply for the grow-your-own grant from MDE, use Q-Comp, realign funds, or find other funding; there is also draft federal legislative funding. This is a two year, elementary program (previously one year). This program is for non-licensed staff with bachelor’s degrees AND teachers who do not have a 5-year standard license (Tier III or IV) who are teaching on a district-requested waiver (Tier I or II). There are at least 25 spots currently open for next year. Coursework will start Fall 2018. Students would retain employment status and would be full-time graduate students. At completion, student would be recommended for licensure summer 2020 and have a masters in education.

Pending its approval by the state, we will soon be offering a cohort for K-12 ESL, mathematics, and sciences. The details of this cohort - other than the pending approval - are the same as the elementary cohort outlined above.

Partnership commitments and details

Financial

District:

  • Year one: 100 hours of release time for staff to complete requirements
  • Year two: pay district stipend for full year student teaching or grant leave of absence for 16 weeks of student teaching in year two or pay salary for teacher of record and benefits

University:

  • Charge a fee in lieu of tuition (36 credits at $600/credit)

Staffing

District:

  • Identify a district liaison who will manage the program internally and serve as the partner representative
  • Facilitate HR and district matters, e.g., leaves, salary, benefits, job placement, induction

University:

  • Fund coordinator, director, advisor, instructors, supervisors, mentor stipends, and administration
  • Ensure all program staff meet CEHD and MnBOT qualifications; staff include adjunct, university faculty, or graduate students

Agreements

District and University:

  • Memorandum of understanding between district and university on deliverables and data sharing
  • Collaborate to recruit and select participants, sites, and mentors
  • Collaborate on legislation and fundraising to support program costs
  • Collaborate on program implementation evaluation and Impact evaluation

Program schedule

Year One: Non-licensed district staff work full-time and complete 18 credits.

Year Two: Three options are available.

  • Leave district staff role and become a full-time teacher candidate (with a paid stipend* as a “resident”) and co-teach for the full year; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits
  • Take a leave of absence for 16 weeks of student teaching; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits
  • Be hired as a teacher of record on a Tier I or Tier II license; mentored and evaluated by university supervisor; complete 18 credits
