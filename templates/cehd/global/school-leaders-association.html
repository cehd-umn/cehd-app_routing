International School Leaders Association

International School Leaders Association brings together education leaders with unique expertise to examine various approaches to promoting the economic and social advancement of minority, refugee and immigrant youth. Participants are primary and secondary school superintendents, principals, head teachers, or district or provincial administrators. The group meets annually in different countries to engage in discussions, action planning, meetings with local educational experts, and visits to schools and community organizations. Their most recent meeting was March 2020 in Germany and Netherlands.

Background

The group came together as part of a US State Department initiative in 2016 as a response to the increase presence of immigrant and refugee students in European school systems. The program focused on knowledge exchange between European countries (Germany, Sweden, Netherlands, France, and U.K.) and the United States around issues of ‘meeting the educational needs of immigrant and refugee students.’ Providing opportunities to learn about existing programs in the US and evolving initiatives in Europe. The first meeting in October 2016 included 20 school administrators from five European countries and several US partners. Since then the group has met four times: January 2017 (France), April 2018 (Germany – Bavaria), March 2019 (U.K. – London and Edinburgh, Scotland), and most recently in Dusseldorf, Germany and Roermond & Alkmaar, Netherlands. These follow up meetings reflect a commitment to the original goals of knowledge exchange and meeting the needs of immigrant and refugee students. Additionally, they reflect the commitment of group participants in establishing a strong and unique international learning community. Fourteen original European school administrators and one US partner have been activity engaged.

General Activities

After the original program in the U.S. and follow up meeting in France (both organized by the US State Department), participants felt it would be important to continue the collaboration. Each annual meeting is organized and led by participants from hosting countries. Activities include: school visits; meetings with students, parents, educators, and education policy makers; discussion circles among participants; cultural site visits; etc. The general purpose of annual meetings is to learn more about the unique educational system of each country and how they have worked to ‘integrate’ immigrants and refugees.

International School Leaders Association Members

France

Marco Brighenti

Marco Brighenti has been a departmental administrator in the Academic Center for Education for New Arrivals and Travelers’ Children (CASNAV), based in Essonne. CASNAV is an expert agency in charge of educating newly arrived students at a regional level in France. The agency is tasked with informing, training, creating, and disseminating educational tools on behalf of teachers, school principals, and inspectors, as well as parents, associations, and social workers. Marco holds a Master’s degree in history and geography. He taught French for two years in Laos and taught history, geography, and citizenship education for 15 years in a suburb of Paris.

Cyril Norbec

Cyril Norbec has worked for the Secretary of State for Education and Science for 16 years. He began his career as a teacher and as a director for students with special needs. Currently, he works as a principal in a Junior High School, and also teaches two classes for disabled pupils and for young migrants.

Germany

Christiane Niedermeier

Christiane Niedermeier - Schulrätin (school inspector) at the Staatliches Schulamt Deggendorf in Niederbayern. Located in the center of Lower Bavaria with approximately 45,000 inhabitants. Her background is also in working with the school exit exam (Abitur).

Martin Oppermann

Martin Oppermann is a consultant at the Ministry of Education and Higher Education in the Federal State of North Rhine-Westphalia. He is responsible for integration programs in regards to the prevention of violence and extremism. Martin is a former teacher of German law at a vocational college. His responsibilities have also included teacher training and providing anti-aggression trainings at schools. He studied law at Ruhr University in Bochum.

The Netherlands

Anna Paulina Smits

Anna Paulina Smits is the principal of Dalton High School in Alkmaar in the Netherlands. Her school has been educating refugee students since 1950. Their aim is to prepare students for the regular curriculum and to facilitate and connect them with native Dutch students.

Regina Buijze

Regina Buijze is a NT2-coach at Vista (vocational school) in Sittard and Maastricht, and expert-teacher Dutch at Agora (high school) in Roermond. She studied modern Dutch literature at the Radboud University in Nijmegen from 2003 to 2005.

The United Kingdom

Graeme Brown

Graeme Brown is a Deputy Rector and also the head of the social subjects faculty of Dunfermline High School, a large Scottish comprehensive secondary school of nearly 1,800 pupils. His degree is in politics and modern history.

John Breslin

John Breslin is head of sociology at Ricard’s Lodge School for girls in London. John works extensively with groups in the Arts that support refugees and other minorities.

Peter Stumpf

Peter Stumpf is an established school leader with experience in both primary and secondary education in London. He is currently head of school at both an elementary and a high school serving highly deprived areas with large numbers of overseas students, many of whom speak little English. In this role, Peter focuses on transforming the community through high quality education.
