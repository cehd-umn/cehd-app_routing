2020 award recipients

The Women’s Philanthropic Leadership Circle is delighted to announce the 2020 recipients:

Graduate awards

Aila O'Loughlin

PhD student, Curriculum and Instruction - Culture and Teaching

Aila’s research focuses on the question: What must teachers know about gender, sex traits and sexuality? For her dissertation, she has interviewed queer teachers and students to produce published work aimed at teacher-candidates, so that future teachers are equipped to create classrooms of safety and belonging for young girls and non-binary adolescents.

“I cannot properly express my gratitude for this award. Thank you for your consideration. I am both honored and humbled. Below is a brief summary of my research toward empowering girls and queer youth in the classroom. Education Sciences has produced arresting statistics on how students’ gender expression impacts their mental health, belonging and safety. This majority quantitative empirical research has in past been a vital way to bring attention to the disparity in access to education that our lgbtqia2+ students face. Despite the growing inclusion of this work into teacher training programs however, 73% of LGBTQ+ youth report being verbally harassed or threatened because of their identities, 95% report having trouble sleeping at night due to these threats and related distress and just five percent of LGBTQ students say that all of their teachers and school staff are supportive of LGBTQ people (Human Rights Campaign Foundation, 2018). Over the past nine months, I have had the opportunity to interview groups of fellow LGBTQIA2+ teachers to discuss our narrative experiences as queer teachers and former youth. In whole, I am interested in "what teachers must know and do when it comes to gender, sex traits, and sexual orientation in the classroom?" And then--how to open up these conversations and further, to teach these best practices in teacher education programs. Thank you so much for this award. This award enables me to write up my findings from this interview project over the course of this summer, to complete my dissertation, and most importantly, to produce text for aspiring teachers that will help build classrooms of belonging for girls, women, queer and trans youth.”

Ndilimeke Nashandi

PhD student, School of Social Work

Ndilimeke’s academic goal is to develop and test culturally responsive child welfare interventions for Namibian girls who experience early pregnancy to ensure that all children and youth have opportunities to improve their social position, and she is conducting her dissertation research with low-income teen mothers in Namibia.

“Among the Aawambo ethnic group in northern Namibia, a southwestern Afrikan country, there is a proverb: ‘kokule iha ku thikwa ngula;’ loosely translated as ‘If you want to go far, don't expect to reach overnight’. This proverb encourages patience, and grit to achieve your vision. I am extremely grateful for the Women's Philanthropic Leadership Circle award and recognition of my vision to address social disparities in Afrika specifically for young women and their families. This award will support the fulfillment of my dissertation, which aims to explore the education opportunities of low-income teen mothers in Namibia. As I conduct this research, the effectiveness of school reengagement for teen mothers will be a focal point. Using an indigenous lens, I plan to explore interventions to enhance the social position of teen mothers. Thank you again for this honor and I look forward to completing my doctoral studies!”

Rising Star Faculty award

Stefanie Marshall

Assistant Professor, Curriculum and Instruction

Watch Stefanie Marshall's video on Zoom

Dr. Marshall has an interest in science education educational policy leadership in science education. Her research has two general strands: 1) the bureaucracy of science and STEM education by examining the role of school leaders, and 2) key stakeholders within science networks. She is a former middle and high school teacher and worked in federally mandated programming in the lowest performing schools in Michigan.

“Thank you for naming me as the recipient of the 2020 Rising Star Award sponsored by the Women’s Philanthropic Leadership Circle (WPLC). I have been blessed to serve communities through my scholarship, teaching, and service. My research centers the impact of science education policy on marginalized students. While science is foregrounded in my work, I am interested in quality and equitable education for all kids. Through courses, I support my students to think critically and to be more socially aware so they can be better teachers for Black and Brown children. Aligned with the mission of WPLC I have empowered women through communities I have played a role in building within CEHD. I am a mentor of four Teacher Scholars of Color, sponsored by C&I, who are prepared to counter the status quo as teachers. I also collaborated in organizing social spaces for Black women in CEHD to sustain one another here at Minnesota. Thank you again for this award, and it will provide me resources to continue supporting equitable education.”
