Commencement speakers

Graduate ceremony keynote speaker

Michael Rodriguez, professor, Department of Educational Psychology, Campbell Leadership Chair in Education and Human Development

Michael Rodriguez, PhD, is the Campbell Leadership Chair in Education and Human Development and a professor of educational psychology in the College of Education and Human Development. He is a fifth-generation Minnesotan and first-generation college graduate, who received his BA from the Morris campus. His research focuses on understanding the psychometric properties of tests—for example, the use of constructed-response versus multiple-choice items. Michael works with school districts to develop methods for improving their use of large-scale test information to improve practices and policies. In addition, he is dedicated to improving the accessibility of assessments of students with disabilities and multilingual learners. Michael leads the Minnesota Youth Development Research Group which helps school districts make use of the Minnesota Student Survey, reporting on positive youth development and social and emotional learning. Michael provides advisory support and leadership to several national organizations, including the National Board of Professional Teaching Standards, National Assessment of Educational Progress, U.S. Department of Defense on military personnel testing, Association of American Medical Colleges, and National Council on Measurement in Education.

Graduate ceremony - provost greetings

Scott Lanyon, vice provost and dean of Graduate Education

Scott M. Lanyon earned his PhD from Louisiana State University in 1985. His research is in the field of avian systematics. Using DNA sequence data, he and his colleagues have developed an understanding of the evolutionary relationships between families, genera and species of birds. They have used that knowledge to shed light on the evolutionary processes responsible for behavioral and morphological patterns observed in birds today. In appreciation of his contributions to ornithology as both scholar and leader, Lanyon was elected president of the American Ornithologists’ Union (2014-2016). Lanyon served as curator of birds at Chicago’s Field Museum of Natural History for 10 years before becoming a faculty member in the Department of Ecology, Evolution, and Behavior in 1995. He served eight years as head of the Department of Ecology, Evolution and Behavior and 13 years as director of the Bell Museum of Natural History. Lanyon was appointed vice provost and dean of graduate education in June 2016. One of his top priorities for graduate education at the University of Minnesota is to prepare students for a wide range of career paths, not only in academia, but also for positions in industry, government, nonprofits, and K-12 education. Other priorities include increasing diversity in graduate education, building communities that welcome students from underrepresented groups, providing training on how to work effectively as members of a diverse team and helping students communicate effectively with diverse audiences.

Undergraduate ceremony keynote speaker

Jill Trites, senior teaching specialist and director of undergraduate studies, Department of Family Social Science

Jill Trites is in her 24th year of teaching at the University of Minnesota where she teaches public speaking and writing-intensive courses in the College of Education and Human Development. She is a recipient of the College of Continuing and Professional Studies Distinguished Teaching Award (2001) and the College of Education & Human Development’s Excellence in Advising Award (2017). Trites' love for teaching undergraduate students motivates her work at the university. In her work as director of undergraduate studies in the Department of Family Social Science, she advises undergraduate majors and works closely with the curriculum. Trites has been dedicated to working with immigrant and refugees at the University of Minnesota, in the state, as well as around the world. She is also a respected advocate for students to have access to higher education and for the benefits of being part of a diverse student body. Trites also volunteers with local refugee resettlement organizations assisting recently arrived families get acclimated to life in Minnesota, delivers groceries from a local foodshelf to East African elders, and conducts teacher training workshops in Mafarinha, Mozambique.


Undergraduate ceremony student speaker

Kania Johnson

Kania Johnson is graduating with a bachelor of science degree in family social science. She is the first person in her family to graduate from a four-year institution. She is currently a TRIO McNair Scholar and student research assistant in the department of Sociology. She has researched black mother-child relationships and racial and ethnic inequalities in the Twin Cities. Kania has been recognized for her research, placing first in 2017 and honorable mention in 2018 at the Black Doctoral Network Conference. Kania is currently serving as a peer-colleague for the TRIO McNair Scholars program and CEHD’s First-Generation Committee. This summer Kania will be an intern for the nonprofit Children of Incarcerated Caregivers and will attend the Humphrey School of Public Affairs to obtain a master’s in public policy.
