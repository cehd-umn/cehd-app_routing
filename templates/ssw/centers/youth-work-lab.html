Tell Me Your World: the Daily Lives of Young People

April 3 and April 10 from 9-12
Where: U of MN St. Paul Campus - Peters Hall (Room 280)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker.
Class fee $75.00
Registration

Relationships are at the heart of youth work. To build a relationship with a young person we must uncover what their daily life is like. Knowing youth differently in this way is a value held by many youth workers, but often challenging to do in busy programs. From the world around us, we also have a host of ideas about “youth” that shape how we perceive, approach and work with youth in communities. Spend time exploring how we think about and construct the idea of “youth”, and how to work to humanize and individualize young people in their particular and social and political context. Connect theory to your own experiences of how you talk and “see” young people in ways that constantly challenge your perceptions. Learn from others new ways to learn more about the youth we spend time with each day.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • A clearer sense of what shapes how you think about young people..
  • Ideas about how to better uncover the daily life experiences of the young people.

Professional Identities in Youth Work

March 13 and March 20 from 9-12
U of MN St. Paul Campus - Student Center (Room 202)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker.
Class fee $75.00
Registration

Each of us has a range of personal beliefs and attitudes that we carry with us to our work. The question is, do they weave together with our professional expectations or work against them? Developing a professional identity allows us to examine our personal and professional selves and find a balance between the two. During this workshop, we will explore and articulate our working philosophies and see how they impact our youth work practices. Spend time with colleagues as you explore the thorny issues of identity in youth work through reading, art work and critical conversations.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Cultivate an understanding of what a professional identity is and how it impacts our work.
  • Gain a deeper understanding of self and self-in-context, examining out personal and professional identities with a critical lens.
  • Understand the implications that professional identity has on our practice and the field of youth work.

Youth in Their Community Contexts

February 27 and March 6 from 9-12
Where: U of MN St. Paul Campus - Peters Hall (Room 280)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker.
Class fee $75.00
Registration

“Beyond the walls of intelligence life is defined” - Nas From ‘New York State of Mind.’

Everyone comes from a variety of different communities. Understanding the worlds that young people belong to and are influenced by will help us gain a better perspective of their everyday lives. This knowledge allows us to build deeper connections with youth and community members. During this workshop, we will dive into the theory of community youth development and critically think about the influences that the community has on its youth, as well as the resources it has to offer. We will explore strong qualities of community programs and practical strategies to become better community builders.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Gain a deeper understanding of the theory of community youth development.
  • Recognize community influences and how they affect the everyday lives of youth.
  • Develop an awareness of strong qualities of community programs and critically reflect on the how your organization connects, supports and encourages relationships and understanding across difference communities.

Therapeutic Trappin’: Rap's value for and on our youth

March 1, 8 and 15 from 10-12
U of MN St. Paul Campus - Student Center (Room 101)
Leo Howard III, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker.
Class fee $75.00
Registration

“Beyond the walls of intelligence life is defined” - Nas From ‘New York State of Mind.’

Finding a way in to the daily lives of young people is not easy – in part because we often do not know nor try to understand what matters to them. Things like clothing, food, types of art, music, or language do not seem relevant or relatable to us. At the same time, youth workers know the power of these things in creating connection and meaning to young people, or even to open up therapeutic or healing space.

Taking up the idea of finding a way in to youth cultures – this class will challenge us to differently understand the art form and history of Rap music. The group will draw connections to the meanings and relevance of Rap through analysis of lyrics and the stories of rappers. We will challenge our biases and notions about Rap music and the artists that create it. Then participants will explore ways Rap expresses ideas about the world that reflect ancient philosophers such as Aristotle while also teaching us about the reality of the young philosophers in our programs.

Finally, the class will discuss how music of any type offers us benefits to our whole being. Then together, make connections to the potential benefits of Rap music for young people as places for relationship, understanding, joy and healing.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • A deeper understanding of themes and ideas inside the works of multiple Rap artists.
  • New ideas about how to draw in Rap music as a way to understand the daily lives of young people.
  • A much cooler soundtrack.

Experiential learning: A four-week applied learning conversation

Fridays, November 9, 16, and 30 and December 7; 10 a.m - noon
Peters Hall, UMN St. Paul Campus
Facilitator: Andrew Goforth, UMN Youth Studies Community Faculty
Class fee $100.00

What is Experiential Learning and how does it impact the everyday lives of young people?

The theory of experiential learning is something often mentioned, sometimes used but rarely deeply explored for the potential to completely shift how we work with young people. This is a deep dive into not just the theory, but how to apply the theory in your sessions with youth each day. This is not your typical workshop but instead acknowledges that change happens over time and by applying and reflecting on our own learning. Take advantage of a powerful and often rare experiential learning space for yourself through a four-week applied learning process.

Why Experiential Learning?

Think back to your days as a young person a child. How did you play? Did you imagine building forts and castles? Did you pretend to be a doctor, teacher or musician? What did these experiences teach you about your self? How did they inform your future experiences? Did you ever think that all this is experiential learning?

We all have experiences every moment of everyday. Are these experiences educational in nature or do they provide a space for learning? The experiences we have define our lives and how we engage with the world in which we live. By revisiting the past and reflecting in the present can we create a future with multiple outcomes and possibilities?

During these conversations/workshops, we will explore the theory and possibilities around Experiential Learning. We will examine the benefits of Experiential Learning and how this can become a positive influence on young people both in their everyday lived lives and in the process of imagining their futures. We will look at the ways in which experiential learning shows up in our everyday lived lives and how we can intentionally use the moments as a space for growing, learning and defining our futures. We will build a bridge between experience and learning.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Gain a deeper understanding of the theory of experiential learning.
  • Begin to put into practice the theoretical ideas that form the foundation of experiential learning.
  • Develop awareness around the possibilities that experiential learning present to young people.
  • Begin to develop ideas, activities and pedagogical approaches around incorporating experiential learning into your youth work practice.

SEL - what is all the buzz about?

Fridays, November 9, 16, and 30 and December 7; 9 a.m.–noon
St. Paul Student Center, Cherrywood Room, UMN St. Paul Campus
Facilitator: Deborah Moore, Youth Work Learning Lab
Class fee $50.00

What is Experiential Learning and how does it impact the everyday lives of young people?

Sometimes it seems like there are new ideas introduced to youth programs every year—all with their own acronym. SEL, or Social Emotional Learning, is not a new concept for people who have been doing work with youth for a while. However, there is new attention and focus on both understanding the practices and how to measure SEL with young people. This session will give you an introduction to SEL and touch on some of the ways we can work towards increasing our focus on it.

In some ways, SEL is working to define the space youth work has always claimed – places for young people to learn in community in ways that are different than traditional learning in school. Join a conversation to:

  • Explore the broader range of SEL skills everyone needs to thrive;
  • Touch on some of the practices that support SEL and may be familiar to you;
  • Explore a set of resources to support your next steps in deepening the use of SEL in your program.

YPQA basics

Friday, December 11, 2018; 9 a.m.–4 p.m.
Peters Hall, UMN St. Paul Campus
Facilitator: Deborah Moore, Youth Work Learning Lab
Class fee $200.00 (lunch included)

The Youth PQA is a research-validated and field-tested tool designed to evaluate the quality of youth programs and identify staff training needs. This hands-on workshop prepares participants to conduct program self-assessment. Since the Youth PQA is an evidence-based assessment tool, it relies on observation and interview. At the training, videos provide an eye into after-school programming and participants get an almost-there experience. Notes and observations are used as evidence to score items.

Who should attend?

Participants can include anyone who will lead or participate in a program self assessment, and may include program staff, funders, and other stakeholders. It is important that participants have an idea of how they will implement the instrument if the training is part of a quality improvement or accountability system. There is some time dedicated to discussing how the instrument fits into local systems, but the bulk of the workshop is focused on learning the instrument itself. This training is also a first step to becoming a reliable external assessor.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Become familiar with the “quality construct” that the Youth PQA represents.
  • Learn the structure of the Youth PQA and how to use & score the instrument.
  • Develop keen observation and note-taking skills to generate rich and precise anecdotal evidence.
  • Apply new skills to score items on a Youth PQA through a consensus meeting.

YPQA reliability for external assessor

Wednesday and Thursday, December 19 and 20, 2018; 9 a.m.–noon
St. Paul Student Center, Cherrywood Room, UMN St. Paul Campus
Facilitator: Deborah Moore, Youth Work Learning Lab
Class fee $500.00 (lunch included)

This course is focused on giving participants video practice and discussion to increase their accuracy in observing and scoring using the Youth Program Quality Assessment Tool (YPQA). It is a great course for those who will be supporting others in the use of the tool in a network or organization, or to ensure that assessors are providing reliable data for use in planning and improvement. Participants who successfully achieve acceptable levels of reliability become Weikart Center endorsed assessors and are able to collect research quality data. This means that assessors are tested and achieve an 80% accuracy rating determined through a video reliability check.

How it works:

All participants must complete YPQA Basics and required homework prior to attending this workshop. If you have not taken this class, you can join the YPQA Basics session on December 11 (see description above), or sign up online through the Weikart Center.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • A deeper understanding of how to observe and score using the YPQA tool.
  • An understanding of areas you need to be more aware of when observing and scoring to hit an accurate rating.

Who should attend

Anyone conducting external assessments as part of a quality assessment and improvement process. Coaches or network advisors that will be working with sites to complete the self-assessment process or to implement improvement plans.
