Youth work learning lab

The Youth Work Learning Lab co-creates learning and research strategies that advance new models for practice and organizational changes that untangle complex and critical issues in youth work.

Current issues being explored: Improving Program Quality, Alternative Models of Evaluation for Youth Programs, Youth Work in Community – Foundational Series, and a variety of Critical Youth Work Practices.

New Series! Youth Work in Community

The Youth Work Learning Lab is launching a new core training series called "Youth Work in Community." The series is designed for youth workers and other related practitioners who want to explore the knowledge, theory and practices that set youth work in community apart from other disciplines. See this page for details.

Current classes

Tell Me Your World: the Daily Lives of Young People

April 3 and April 10, 9 a.m.–noon
Where: U of MN St. Paul Campus - Peters Hall (Room 280)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker
Class fee $75.00
Registration

Relationships are at the heart of youth work. To build a relationship with a young person we must uncover what their daily life is like. Knowing youth differently in this way is a value held by many youth workers, but often challenging to do in busy programs. From the world around us, we also have a host of ideas about “youth” that shape how we perceive, approach and work with youth in communities. Spend time exploring how we think about and construct the idea of “youth”, and how to work to humanize and individualize young people in their particular and social and political context. Connect theory to your own experiences of how you talk and “see” young people in ways that constantly challenge your perceptions. Learn from others new ways to learn more about the youth we spend time with each day.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • A clearer sense of what shapes how you think about young people.
  • Ideas about how to better uncover the daily life experiences of the young people.

Professional Identities in Youth Work

March 13 and March 20, 9 a.m.–noon
U of MN St. Paul Campus - Student Center (Room 202)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker
Class fee $75.00
Registration

Each of us has a range of personal beliefs and attitudes that we carry with us to our work. The question is, do they weave together with our professional expectations or work against them? Developing a professional identity allows us to examine our personal and professional selves and find a balance between the two. During this workshop, we will explore and articulate our working philosophies and see how they impact our youth work practices. Spend time with colleagues as you explore the thorny issues of identity in youth work through reading, art work and critical conversations.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Cultivate an understanding of what a professional identity is and how it impacts our work.
  • Gain a deeper understanding of self and self-in-context, examining out personal and professional identities with a critical lens.
  • Understand the implications that professional identity has on our practice and the field of youth work.

Youth in Their Community Contexts

February 27 and March 6 from 9-12
Where: U of MN St. Paul Campus - Peters Hall (Room 280)
Deborah Moore, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker
Class fee $75.00
Registration

“Beyond the walls of intelligence life is defined” - Nas From ‘New York State of Mind.’

Everyone comes from a variety of different communities. Understanding the worlds that young people belong to and are influenced by will help us gain a better perspective of their everyday lives. This knowledge allows us to build deeper connections with youth and community members. During this workshop, we will dive into the theory of community youth development and critically think about the influences that the community has on its youth, as well as the resources it has to offer. We will explore strong qualities of community programs and practical strategies to become better community builders.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • Gain a deeper understanding of the theory of community youth development.
  • Recognize community influences and how they affect the everyday lives of youth.
  • Develop an awareness of strong qualities of community programs and critically reflect on the how your organization connects, supports and encourages relationships and understanding across difference communities.

Therapeutic Trappin’: Rap's value for and on our youth

March 1, 8 and 15 from 10-12
U of MN St. Paul Campus - Student Center (Room 101)
Leo Howard III, University of Minnesota Youth Development Leadership MEd Alumni and Youth Worker
Class fee $75.00
Registration

“Beyond the walls of intelligence life is defined” - Nas From ‘New York State of Mind.’

Finding a way in to the daily lives of young people is not easy – in part because we often do not know nor try to understand what matters to them. Things like clothing, food, types of art, music, or language do not seem relevant or relatable to us. At the same time, youth workers know the power of these things in creating connection and meaning to young people, or even to open up therapeutic or healing space.

Taking up the idea of finding a way in to youth cultures – this class will challenge us to differently understand the art form and history of Rap music. The group will draw connections to the meanings and relevance of Rap through analysis of lyrics and the stories of rappers. We will challenge our biases and notions about Rap music and the artists that create it. Then participants will explore ways Rap expresses ideas about the world that reflect ancient philosophers such as Aristotle while also teaching us about the reality of the young philosophers in our programs.

Finally, the class will discuss how music of any type offers us benefits to our whole being. Then together, make connections to the potential benefits of Rap music for young people as places for relationship, understanding, joy and healing.

What You'll Have When You Walk Away:

  • A deeper understanding of themes and ideas inside the works of multiple Rap artists.
  • New ideas about how to draw in Rap music as a way to understand the daily lives of young people.
  • A much cooler soundtrack.
