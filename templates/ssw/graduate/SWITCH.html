Social Work Interprofessional Training Collaborative for Health (SWITCH) Fellowship

The Social Work Interprofessional Training Collaborative for Health (SWITCH) fellowship program provides a one-time stipend of $8,000 for specialized training that prepares students to provide behavioral health services. It is not intended for persons who only seek advanced training and experience in psychotherapy. An important component of this fellowship is for students to acquire the knowledge and skills that will prepare them to work effectively in interprofessional contexts. A minimum of 15 fellowships will be awarded for the 2020-2021 academic year 2020-2021.

Eligibility

The student must be in either the Clinical Mental Health (CMH), Families and Children (FC), or Health, Disability, and Aging (HDA) concentration.

The student must be enrolled in either SW 8020 or SW 8030 in fall 2020 and spring 2021. (Summer block field placements are not eligible.

The student must have a field placement in which they provide behavioral health services. Important note: Because this award requires a placement at an approved field agency, confirmation of the SWITCH fellowship award does not occur until AFTER the field matching process in May.

Application

Applicants must complete the SWITCH Application, which includes a short essay.

All students who are applying for this fellowship should also complete the 2020-2021 Social Work Student Financial Aid Form.

Special requirements

Students who accept a SWITCH fellowship award must agree to the following conditions:

  • CMH and FC concentration students must select HDA as their second anchor.
  • HDA concentration students must select CMH as their second anchor.
  • Students must agree to enroll in SW 5903–Substance Use and Social Work as one of their elective courses.
  • Students must agree to take part in a no-credit seminar that meets a minimum of two (2) times per semester for approximately 1.5 to 2.0 hours each session.

Please see the application for additional terms and conditions.
