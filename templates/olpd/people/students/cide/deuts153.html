Bio

My research focuses on the whiteness of the teaching force and the ways that white educators uphold, and potentially confront, racism and white supremacy in their classrooms. In particular, I use Critical Whiteness Studies as a framework to understand the nuanced and intersectional factors that contribute to how white educators make sense of their identities, both personally and in a professional context. I am very passionate about education and I have worked in the field for over a decade. Much of my experience is in the K-12 classroom. Prior to joining the CIDE program, I taught second grade and middle school both internationally and locally. I now teach undergraduate and graduate level courses at the University of Minnesota; my courses include:

  • CI 1001 - Introduction to the Elementary School
  • CI 5283 - Practicum: Applying Instructional Methods in Elementary Schools
  • CI 5285 - Clinical Experience in Elementary Teaching
  • OLPD 2811 - Societies of the Future: Changing Work Contexts
  • OLPD 5009 - Human Relations: Applied Skills for School & Society
In addition to teaching, I also supervise teaching candidates in their field placements for the Department of Curriculum and Instruction. After completing my PhD, I hope to continue my work with future educators by pursuing an academic position in a teacher education program.
