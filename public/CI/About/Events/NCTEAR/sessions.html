NCTE Assembly for Research Mid-Winter Conference

"Teaching and Researching Across Color Lines: Literacies, Pedagogies, and the Politics of Difference"

Paper Sessions

Roundtable Session 1: Saturday, February 22, 2003 9:40 a.m.-10:25 a.m.

1.1 Stephen M. Fishman, University of North Carolina Charlotte, Lucille McCarthy, University of Maryland Baltimore County: Dialogic Research Report Forms: Representing Conflict in a Study of Underprepared Writers

In our two-part presentation, we discuss our recently completed study of underprepared writers in courses across the curriculum. In this research, a philosopher and a composition researcher collaborate to study high-status and low-status minority students in the former's philosophy courses. We focus our remarks on the following questions: How did our research with socially marginalized groups help us (1) look critically at writing pedagogies in content-area courses and (2) reconsider the ways we represent our research?

Location: Faculty

1.2 Kristi Rudelius-Palmer, University of Minnesota Human Rights Center; Tonya McClary, American Friends Service Committee; Judge Lajune Lange, International Institute and Human Rights Center; Amalia Anderson, Human Rights Center: International Treaties and Documents: A New Lens Through Which to Teach Others

One significant challenge facing any education system is to develop teaching practices that foster learning among all human beings and have equal success with persons of all cultures. Many communities in this country do not do a good job educating members of minority cultures. International human rights treaties serve as a potentially invaluable resource for educators when formulating educational policies and curricula because they appeal to more than just one dominant culture. It is therefore suggested that international human rights treaties/documents be used as the new lens through which educators view their role in teaching our children and young adults.

Location: Alumni

1.3 Jennifer Seibel Trainor, University of Pittsburgh: Persuaded by Race: Toward a Method for Reading Whiteness in the Critical Classroom

Using data collected in an all-white suburban high school, I make a two-pronged argument about white racism and anti-racist pedagogy. First, I argue that current representations of whiteness in educational literature delimit the rhetorical and subjective possibilities for white students in critical classrooms. Secondly, I analyze racist discourse as it occurred in the high school, examining the multiple meanings hidden beneath its reductive political clarity. Such analysis, I argue, helps explain the persuasive coherence of, and thus white investment in, the rhetoric of race and racism.

Location: Regents

1.4 Mitzi Lewison, Indiana University, Lee Heffernan, Childs Elementary and Indiana University: Separate but Not Equal: Literacy Practices and Power Relationships in a Third Grade Classroom

This study examines how students represented the tensions, contradictory outcomes, potential loss of freedom, and benefits of taking on a local "lunchroom desegregation" project and how gender interacted with these outcomes. The roundtable discussion is intended to extend understandings of how critical literacy practices can create sites of repositioning that expand as well as restrict students' abilities to assert collective power to redesign the social spaces they inhabit in school.

Location: Presidents

1.5 Linda Williams, Michigan State University and Detroit Waldorf School, Susan Florio-Ruane, Michigan State University and Center for Improvement of Early Reading Achievement: Critical Literacy and the Path into Teaching: Historical Teacher Biographies and Racial Dynamics in New York and Detroit (1870-1920)

We examine the paths to teaching and school literacy of women from two groups new to public education at turn of the Nineteenth Century: African American migrants to Detroit from the rural South, and immigrants to New York City from the Campania of Southern Italy. Using comparative, interpretive methods, we develop narrative "teaching life compositions." We use primary and secondary sources in a variety of genres including fiction, autobiography, oral history, and social science texts.

Location: Collegiate

1.6 Thandeka Chapman, University of Wisconsin, Milwaukee: Stopping at the Edge of the Cliff: A White Teacher’s Attempts at Discussions of Race, Class, and Gender in an English Class with a Multi-Ethnic, Multi-Racial Student Population

This presentation focuses on pedagogical issues concerning the implementation of libratory/ anti-racist pedagogy in multi-ethnic classrooms. Through the lens of Critical Race Theory, I explore race and social class configurations that impact the teacher’s pedagogical choices and the students’ affective responses to classroom events. These explorations are situated within the etic contexts of city and district desegregation, as well as the emic contexts of classroom milieu and interpersonal relationships.

Location: Rotary

1.7 Stacy Miller, University of New Mexico: Cultural Heuristic Writing Assessment in the Secondary Language Arts Classroom

This study argues that teachers are "mis"-prepared to recognize discourse patterns in student writing, and as a result often mis-assess student papers in the secondary language arts classroom. What is often assessed as standard error to a teacher reflects their misunderstandings of cultural discourse patterns. Findings from the study revealed that the teacher who intuits or consciously presumes the intersectionality between assignment development and assessment was able to more acutely assess students as they wove home discourse and "standard English" in the writing process.

Location: Nolte

1.8 Bernadine Skowronski, University of Cincinnati: A Circle of Culture In A Municipal Housing Authority Neighborhood

This round table will introduce a participatory action research project that was guided by the philosophy of Paulo Freire and analyzed through the lenses of Critical Race Theory and privilege. It will begin a dialogue of how the elements of CRT and privilege can be applied to basic adult education settings and how Freire's ideas of liberatory education can be used to make this a reality.

Location: Northrop

1.9 Lisa Albrecht, University of Minnesota: Anti-Racism as Visible Action: Teaching and Writing about Whiteness

Why might it be in the best interest of white people to work primarily with each other (as well as with people of color) to eradicate racism? White educator activists have a rich, though buried, history of white people who have worked for racial justice. White people do have role models, and we need to learn of them. This paper discusses how white educators can draw upon our white antiracist histories to educate ourselves and our students about ways to fight racism.

Location: Coffman

1.10 Sharon M. Keller, Bowling Green State University: Within a Framework of Literacy Orientations: Understanding Literacy Orientations and Teaching

This paper presentation arises from a larger dissertation piece researching women teacher's literacies, beliefs, ideologies and practices examined from a framework grounded within critical theory and poststructuralism. In seeking to address conceptually and pedagogically the question regarding the preparation of teachers to take up the racial dimensions of reading, writing, and oral language in classrooms, I will present, suggest, and invite conversation toward the use of critical orientation frameworks as a process that reveals and undertakes issues of difference within the classroom.

Location: Campus

Roundtable Session 2: Saturday, February 22, 2003 10:35 a.m.-11:20 a.m

2.1 Mary Louis Gomez, University of Wisconsin-Madison; Jennifer Stone, University of Wisconsin-Madison; Nikola Hobbel, University of Wisconsin-Madison: Textual Tactics of Identification: How do Middle School Students Use Literacy to Resist Whiteness?

This paper examines students’ resistance of the whiteness of schooling through textual practices. Based on a yearlong ethnographic study in an eighth grade remedial reading class, we examine how whiteness was imposed through state and district policies, assessment practices, classroom pedagogies, and textual selection. Likewise, through the lens of “textual tactics of identification,” we examine how students use reading, writing, and media to subvert the whiteness of schooling.

Location: Faculty

2.2 Leon Rodrigues, University of Minnesota: Developing Critical Multicultural Practice: An Action Approach

Critical pedagogy can be viewed as an educational process of constructing awareness, knowledge and authentic practice that leads to meaningful engagement across boundaries of difference and power, alienation and cooperation, legitimacy and exclusion. This critical inquiry process included thematic analysis, dialogue and reflection. The goal was to increase critical consciousness and help agitate for changes that would benefit clients from diverse cultural backgrounds.

Location: Alumni

2.3 Lesley A. Rex, University of Michigan: Exploring Relationships among Identity Performance, Social Relationship Building, and Expressions of Raced Culture in Acts of Teaching and Learning

Through this exploratory study of discourse between high school English teachers and students of same and different races, I explore race as a complex dimension integrally related to other dimensions of classroom teaching and learning. These are interrelationships among individual identity performances, social relationship building, and subject matter construction. I focus on what counts as ?the problem? as negotiated in the discourse of the interactants. 

Location: Regents

2.4 Jane Bolgatz, Fordham University, Bruce Fehn, University of Iowa: Using Primary Sources to Open Conversations about Race in American History

What happens when a researcher, a teacher, and a class of fourth graders decide to look at the multicultural history of New York State in order to answer the question: How did we get here? This research examines classroom conversations about primary documents and the process that the teacher, students and researcher experienced as they entered into conversations about race in American history.

Location: Presidents

2.5 Caroline T. Clark, Ohio State University: Creating Classrooms that Support Difference and Community: Learning from Extra-Curricular Sites of Difference

This presentation draws on empirical data from three research studies to explore conceptual questions of how differences - including and beyond those of race, class, and gender - affect the creation of community in extra-curricular sites. Working in extra-curricular sites revealed a range of differences -- differences which may not be readily apparent in conventional classroom sites, but which have consequences for how teaching and learning in a critical vein might occur.

Location: Collegiate

2.6 Megan Madigan Peercy, University of Utah: Powerful Discourses in the Preparation and Practices of ESL Teachers

Many scholars have critiqued the preparation of teachers working with English language learners (ELLs). Using a Foucauldian theoretical approach, this paper adds to the discussion on the preparation of ESL (English as a second language) teachers, arguing that there is a remarkable lack of attention to the relationship between language and power in most second language teacher education literature, and that such inattention can affect pedagogy in important ways.

Location: Rotary

2.7 Phyllis Balcerzak, Washington University in St. Louis; Margaret Finders, Washington University in St. Louis; Diane Hui, Washington University in St. Louis; Elizabeth Malancharuvil-Berkes, Washington University in St. Louis: Reading through the Color Lines: A Critical Examination of Race in the Journal of Teacher Education

How do teacher educators take up race in their research and practice? Drawing on research into the Journal of Teacher Education, we offer a lens onto normative conceptions and representations that may serve to define the field. Since the 1990’s the increasing diversity of school children and an increase in difficulties inherent in the educational system have converged requiring reforms within teacher education. In this session, we address how such reforms and revisions are represented in the texts of teacher education.

Location: Nolte

2.8 Jeff Park, University of Saskatchewan: Expressive Writing: Embracing Diversity and Opportunity

My presentation focuses on the personal writing of Albert Chatsis, a sixty-eight year old Cree man who has been socially and economically marginalized. Albert's written voice demonstrates that writing in the expressive function creates a metaphoric space of great diversity -- a riparian zone. In this space, Albert constructs a sense of self, and then negotiates relationships with others and the world through the creation of the written texts.

Location: Northrop

2.9 Sarah Edwards, University of Nebraska, Omaha: Overcoming Simplification Strategies: Moving Beliefs into Action

Preservice teachers are often left to assume how to place theoretical concepts into action. Qualitative case study methodology was used to understand how a structured service-learning experience may impact preservice teachers understandings of culturally relevant teaching. When confronted with diversity issues in the classroom, these preservice teachers were provided with scaffolding to go beyond simplified teaching strategies for culturally responsive instruction.

Location: Campus

2.10 Fiona Glade, Washington State University: Multiliteracies Matter: Genre as Motive

This roundtable will encourage participants to interrogate the ways in which texts in various genres--not just those in English studies--are categorized. Following a brief overview of some applications of critical pedagogy and genre theory, we will explore K-12 and college classroom strategies that foster a critical literacy through which students and teachers may examine the motivations of the various contexts and texts that we consume and produce in classes across the disciplines.

Location: Campus

Roundtable Session 3: Saturday, February 22, 2003 3:10 p.m.-3:55 p.m.

3.1 Patrick Bruch, University of Minnesota; Patricia James, University of Minnesota; Rashné Jehangir, University of Minnesota: Learning and Unlearning Community: Recomposing Agency in a Predominantly White University

Panelists will discuss theoretical underpinnings, curricular materials, and student projects from a three-course learning community that serves first-generation, low-income students in a predominantly white public research university. In the learning community that we will analyze, students enroll as a group in creative arts, multicultural relations, and first-year writing. We will explain and assess our efforts to help students compose a sense of confidence, belonging, and transformative purpose in a predominantly white institution of higher education.

Location: Faculty

3.2 Sherry Marx, University of California and Fontana Unified School District: Racism, Whiteness and White Pre-Service Teachers Tutoring English Language Learners of Color

This critical qualitative study examines the racially and culturally biased beliefs that nine white, female students in a teacher education class constructed about English language learning children of color they tutored during a semester-long field service experience. Methodology included in-depth, open-ended interviews, observations of tutoring sessions, and collection of participant tutoring journals. The need to take a critical theory perspective in teacher preparation courses devoted to language and cultural diversity is centered in this paper/presentation.

Location: Alumni

3.3 Cynthia Lewis, University of Iowa; Jean Ketter, Grinnell College: Resituating the Self: Interdiscursivity in a Teacher and Researcher Study Group

This paper analyzes discussions of multicultural literature among white middle school teachers in a rural setting. Using critical discourse analysis, we examine how participants take up aspects of each others' world views, patterns of talk, and systems of thought as they relate to multicultural literature. These interdiscursive moments have implications for a theory of learning as social interaction and for the professional development of teachers in long-term informal settings.

Location: Regents

3.4 Heather Harding, Harvard University: “City Girl”: A Portrait of one White teacher of inner city students of color

When considering effective teaching for students of color, much of the research discusses the work of teachers of color. While White educators have offered narratives on anti-racist education, little research has taken an empirical look at successful White teachers of students of color. This study focuses on understanding the motivations of one such teacher and how her meaning making of race and her own racial identity inform her pedagogy, goals, and practice.

Location: Presidents

3.5 Haesook Koo, University of Minnesota: Negotiating Ethnic Identities Across Color Lines: A Comparative Study of Bi-racial and Adopted Korean Americans in Minnesota

This is a comparative study of bi-racial and adopted Korean Americans in Minnesota to explore the complexity and malleability of ethnic identity. The study utilizes an “ethnographically-informed” approach (Wolcott, 1999) to offer holistic and in-depth explanations through a combination of ethnographic interviews, life histories, and the Intercultural Development Inventory (IDI). The findings will provide insights and understandings about the complex issues of ethnic identity development and effects of educational experiences on ethnic identity formation.

Location: Collegiate

3.6 Jeff Duncan-Andrade, University of California, Los Angeles; Ernest Morrell, Michigan State University: E“race”ing, Enabling: Toward a Critical Media Pedagogy in Secondary Literacy Classrooms

In order to function as empowered citizens urban teens of color need to deconstruct and reinterpret the messages that are sent to them through the mass media. Critical citizens must also be producers of media texts writing or using other images when and where they can in the cause of social justice. Duncan-Andrade and Morrell argue that the media provide an ideal context for teachers to develop reading and writing for empowerment. Drawing upon their research and work with urban teens of color, they provide several suggestions for using the media to develop academic and critical literacies in urban classrooms.

Location: Rotary

3.7 Brenda G. Juarez, University of Utah: Crossing Color Lines in Dual Language Immersion Education: An Empirical Study

Dual language education programs, increasingly popular, are widely cited as a promising means of successfully educating language minority children. Yet little is known about the consequences of differentially valued social relations upon the educational and social outcomes for participating children. This empirical study examines the lived experiences of children in a dual language classroom for the purpose of illuminating possibilities for realizing dialogical movement between the margins.

Location: Nolte

3.8 Gail Boldt, University of Iowa, Cynthia Miller, The University of Iowa: Is Love Enough? Identity and Desire in Learning and Teaching

In this session, we explore issues raised for us as the white mothers of children who are ethnically and religiously different than we are. In particularly, we will explore the contours of our own investments in our children's identities as "not white" and ask what this understanding can suggest about learning and teaching in school and university classrooms. For this work we draw from psychoanalytic, post-structural, feminist, and post-colonial theories.

Location: Northrop

3.9 Yolanda Sealey-Ruiz, New York University, Avon Cowell, New York University: Writing Black: Explorations of Writing from the Elementary to the Postsecondary Classroom of African American students

Are educators and activists interested in centering the voices and experiences of Black students across and educational continuum? "Writing Black" supports the notion that Culturally Relevant Teaching (CRT) creates meaningful classroom discourse and writing experiences for African American secondary and postsecondary students. Elementary and adult reentry student case studies are used as a catalyst for an open and honest discussion around literacy issues facing Black students.

Location: Coffman

3.10 Hans Skott-Myhre, University of Minnesota: Culturally Sensitive Evaluation: A Proposal

This paper focuses on the conceptual/theoretical questions surrounding culturally sensitive evaluation and addresses the question: How can research with racially marginalized groups generate a critique of our research methodologies and pedagogies? Possible reasons for the paucity of literature in the field of culturally sensitive evaluation, based in particular kinds of knowledge production will be outlined. Recommendations will be made about how the field of evaluation and the evaluator might engage culturally sensitive work more successfully.

Location: Campus

Roundtable Session 4: Saturday, February 22, 2003 4:05 p.m.-4:50 p.m.

4.1 Sara Michael Luna, University of Wisconsin, Madison: Why ‘I’m Sorry’ Isn’t the Same as Lo Siento’: Identity and Code-Switching in a Spanish-Dominant First Grade

This paper documents one teacher’s journey toward the development of a supportive literacy classroom which promotes the academic and linguistic development of his Latino students. It also furthers our understanding of the role of language in the academic development and engagement of English language learners, and explores the efficacy of current methods of analyzing language-in-use by examining how relationships between language, culture and identity impact the learning and schooling of linguistically diverse populations.

Location: Faculty

4.2 Elizabeth Capifali, University of Massachusetts, Amherst: Unmasking Discourses: High School Students Weaving In and Out of Power Relationships in Educational Settings

This study uses in-depth interviews of an ethnically diverse group of high school students to explore their interactions as they grappled with issues of marginalization, discrimination and racism in their school. Discourse analysis provided a tool for studying the impact that Discourse had on their positionality within the group. Because schools are sites for learning, as well as arenas of power and struggle, students’ Discourses and teacher’s interpretation of language and behavior can ultimately determine the involvement or marginalization of students.

Location: Alumni

4.3 Stephanie Carter, Indiana University: “…Always about a White person”: Examining Whiteness in a Secondary British Literature Classroom

This paper presents findings from an ethnographic study in a public high school British Literature classroom with only two African-American females. Findings showed that African-American girls in predominately White classroom environments reading predominately White Literature are constantly negotiating “silencing” in the curriculum as related to their identities. As a result of their negotiation of “silencing,” they create spaces to support each other. This study is significant because it examines unacknowledged, connected spaces of agency between adolescent African-American females as well as examines areas of curriculum that create difficulties related to issues of race.

Location: Regents

4.4 Cathy Compton-Lilly, Rochester City School District & St. John Fisher College: Former Students, Four Years Later: The Literacy Learning of Urban First Grade Students Across Time and Place

A teacher researcher will present case studies of two of her former first grade students and their families. These case studies will focus on how these students are positioned in terms of their reading success and failure both at home and in school. A theoretical exploration of "reading capital" will be presented to explore the complexities of reading success and failure.

Location: Presidents

4.5 Michael Riendau, Eagle Hill School, Hardwick, MA, Eric Stone, Eagle Hill School, Hardwick, MA: White. Right? Examining White Privilege in the Secondary English Classroom

In this session, two white, straight, male secondary English teachers discuss their ongoing action research project that examines their experiences in teaching “New Traditions in American Literature,” a course that addresses literature from oppressed and historically underrepresented groups. Topics for discussion include questions of authenticity, the construction of authority, and the development and defense of a course curriculum.

Location: Collegiate

4.6 Cynthia Williams, Vanderbilt University: “I’m gon be me”: African American Males Negotiating the use of African American Language in a Developmental College Writing Class

This paper presents findings from an ethnographic study in two developmental writing classes at an urban community college. Data were collected over five months in 2002. Findings showed that: 1) The tension between AAL and the language affirmed in the classroom is known to the students and it is a topic they reflect upon and 2) the context of the composition class provides no “space” for discussion of language variation other than to insist on Standard English. The research suggests that researchers may have overlooked the various efforts students make to create spaces for the negotiation of language.

Location: Rotary

4.7 David E. Kirkland, Michigan State University; Austin Jackson, Michigan State University: Toward A Critical Language Pedagogy

Our presentation demonstrates how critical language pedagogy can alter students’ attitudes toward language and increase proficiency in multiple literacies by affirming and teaching academic English and students’ home language as equal entities. Findings indicate that students instructed in ways that affirm both academic English and US Ebonics develop positive attitudes toward language and literacy. Data suggests that students developed greater proficiency in multiple linguistic codes and could negotiate situations by electing the appropriate language for a given context.

Location: Nolte

4.8 Joyce Bainbridge, University of Alberta; Nicole Green, University of Alberta: Transcending Homogeneous Beliefs and Understandings through Canadian Children’s Literature

Educators are increasingly aware that the values, attitudes and ideas children receive through literature (as well as through other media) are powerful influences on how children view themselves (and their role) in the society to which they belong. This session explores some of the issues facing Canadian teachers who, in focusing on Canadian identity and culture, find themselves struggling to overcome the dominance of American books in their schools.

Location: Northrop

4.9 Luisa Araujo, William Paterson University: Confronting Prejudice in the Early Childhood Classroom

This presentation focuses on ways to confront prejudice and celebrate diversity in the early childhood classroom. Different levels of awareness are discussed taking the practices of one teacher as a starting point. In addition, this presentation offers suggestions on how to incorporate other teaching-learning practices and materials to help all children celebrate diversity.

Location: Coffman

4.10 Steven Bialostok, University of Wyoming: An Elite Racist Register: The Explanatory System of One White, Middle-Class Woman

This paper describes an empirical study of one speaker’s unmarked normative assumptions made during an interview. I explore how covert racist ideologies are made concrete and publicly accessible through discourse — in this case, of an elite racialized register. Particularly relevant is how this speaker — a college educated White, middle-class mother of a young child — is married to a Latino whose family lives in a large city in the Southwest, near the Mexico border.

Location: Campus

Roundtable Session 5: Sunday, February 23, 2003 10:05 a.m.-10:50 a.m.

5.1 Mark Schultz, Lewis University: What is Education For? The Southern Sharecropper’s Dilemma, 1900-1950

Among the poor people of black majority, rural Hancock County, Georgia, no consensus existed on the value of education from 1900-1945. Some promoted education, hoping it would give their children leverage in dealings with landlords. Others dismissed it as impractical. Only when migrants left their premodern, mule-based farms for the cities did education reveal its worth. This study is based on county education records and 200 interviews with elderly people.

Location: Faculty

5.2 Beth Giles, University of Wisconsin, Madison: Confessions of A White Savior Teacher: A Journey from Saving to Teaching

The focus of the discussion will be comprehending the mind set of a "white savior" teacher, examining how teachers can become aware of their whiteness and whether or not the savior mentality can be transformed to one of respectful, successful, culturally relevant practice. To understand this transformation the speaker will reflect on her own practice as a white teacher situated in an urban setting as well as discuss literature and media representations relevant to the topic. Additionally, the presenter seeks to study the effects of the "white savior" mentality on urban students.

Location: Alumni

5.3 Brian Edmiston, The Ohio State University: “Refractive” Practitioner Research in a Polarized Community

I analyze discourse from teacher-research that I conducted in ethnically polarized Northern Ireland classrooms where I used classroom drama as pedagogy in Citizenship Education classes.  I contrast my "reflections" with my "refractive" analysis in order to theorize refractive practitioner research.  I illustrate that refractive research can make more visible for practitioners how their beliefs and values influence their teaching decisions.

Location:  Regents

5.4 Stacie L. Tate, Michigan State University: A Responsive Intervention: Exploring How Teachers Conceptualize the Relationship between Subject Matter and the Racial and Ethnic Experience of Their Students

This presentation focuses on the empirical question of the teachers’ conceptions of the relationship between subject matter and the racial and ethnic experiences of students in the classroom. Further, this presentation challenges the nature of teacher training, and analyzes a curricular intervention designed to enable more critical and culturally responsive practitioners.

Location: Presidents

5.5 Rebecca Rogers, Washington University in St. Louis; Jennifer Hutchinson, Washington University in St. Louis; Anne Ellis, Washington University in St. Louis: From Writing Sermons to Reading Basal Texts: A Critical Discourse Analysis of Literate Identities Across Contexts

This paper reports on an interview study designed to study the relationship between linguistic resources, identities, and contexts. The participants of the study were African American adults enrolled in literacy programs who have children in public schools. Using critical discourse analysis, we demonstrate the relationship between “ways of interacting”, “ways of representing”, and “ways of being” in the adults’ history of participation with school, their children’s school experiences, and in their family and community literacy practices.

Location: Collegiate

5.6 Laura Renzi Miller, The Ohio State University: Beginning the Conversation: Using Multicultural Adolescent Literature with Future Teachers

This presentation focuses on undergraduate students’ participation in conversations around race, class, and gender within an Adolescent Literature class.  Analysis indicates that these undergraduates bring with them very definite beliefs about what should and should not be discussed within a classroom setting and what a teacher's role is in these conversations.  This study focuses on how students choose to participate within this conversation and how adolescent literature can be used to create a space where students feel comfortable expressing ideas about literature.

Location: Rotary

5.7 TOCAR Institutional Assessment Committee, Concordia College, Minnesota State University Moorhead, North Dakota State University, Northwest Technical College: Building Anti-Racist Institutions: The Use of Institutional Assessment for the Construction Process (Phyllis May-Machunda, Minnesota State University Moorhead, Deborah White, Minnesota State University Moorhead, Amy Phillips, Minnesota State University Moorhead, Richard Chapman, Concordia College)

This roundtable session will provide participants the opportunity to examine and discuss a survey instrument designed to help four educational institutions in Fargo, North Dakota and Moorhead, Minnesota assess campus and community climate, institutional racism, and perceptions and experiences of discrimination. The survey, developed by the presenters, is being administered as part of an initiative that seeks to build multicultural, anti-racist learning environments within the context of higher education.

Location: Nolte

5.8 Hine Waitere-Ang, University of Wisconsin, Madison: Fair/Fare or Fear Trading? Insider Trading – Whiteness in the Market Place

Feeding the publicly imagined space can no longer be seen simply as the prerogative of white privilege. Who, how and to what end different groups get invested in the public imagination are critical questions that must be constantly posed and reposed to maintain a critical collective consciousness. To this end, this paper examines advertisements produced to market Aotearoa/New Zealand internationally during the millennium celebrations to see how whiteness is inscribed on the indigenous body.

Location: Northrop

5.9 Mary Kay Rummel, University of Minnesota, Duluth and California State University at Channel Islands; Elizabeth Quintero, New York University: Problem Posing Methodology for Diversity Education Presented through Literature and Action

In a presentation which combines theory with qualitative research, we propose to focus upon problem posing as a way to prepare teachers to take up the racial and global dimensions of literacy in classrooms through a Listening, Dialogue, Action format. This presentation is based on the results of a research project that connects critical theory, critical literacy, integrated arts, multicultural literature and autobiography. We will then show how this approach is used with preservice teachers in order to help them understand the complexities involved when thinking about culture and language.

Location: Coffman

5.10 Richard Beach, University of Minnesota; Amanda Haertling, University of Minnesota; Daryl Parks, University of Minnesota: High School Students' Responses to Portrayals of Race, Class, and Gender Conflicts in Multicultural Literature

This study examined 12th grade students' discussion and written responses to portrayals of conflicts in eight multicultural novels related to race, class, and gender. Data were coded for teacher strategies; discourses of race, class, and gender; and key tensions in discussions. The teacher fostered student interrogation of discourses of race, class, and gender through serving as a mediator/negotiator of competing worlds and demonstrator of critical lenses. Tensions often revolved around students' conflicted attitudes towards affirmative action, white privilege, and exclusionary practices in the school.

Location: Campus
