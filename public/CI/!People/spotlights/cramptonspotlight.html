Literacy Education Ph.D. Candidate, Anne Crampton

 

Anne Crampton, a fourth-year Ph.D. student in Literacy Education received the Women’s Philanthropic Leadership Circle (WPLC) award for graduate students. The award is for women graduate students to recognize their achievements and successes in their field of interest. The criteria for the award includes academic achievements, community involvement, leadership, and passion for the academic and professional career of choice.

Crampton’s research focus is in secondary critical literacy where she is currently looking at the student experience in both a large, urban high school and a small, urban charter school. “I think it is important to show how very different students’ schooling experiences shape them. There can be negative stereotypes assigned to large, urban schools because people often don’t see excellent work being done in those classrooms,” Crampton says.

After 15 years as a teacher in public schools, Crampton pursued her Ph.D. in Literacy Education to have a better understanding of what shapes the education system and the root of inequity I the classroom. “Certain things kept me awake at night about what I didn’t think was fair or right. I wanted to understand it and be a part of the conversation.”

Crampton’s Ph.D. studies have helped her make more sense of some of the arguments in public education and the urgency around them. She feels there are very positive and effective education techniques that offer the chance for a transformative learning experience.” I’d like other people to know that it does happen and it’s possible. People want to hear about it in three words, but it’s complicated. Implementing effective education techniques takes support, an excellent teacher, flexibility, and the support of district. “

Crampton is particularly focused on creating more emotional connections in the classroom through “aesthetic experiences.” That means big projects that students can have a creative stake, not a mere repetitive learning exercise, that allows an aspect of performance, be it a podcast or a play. “I think you can do all those things in many different disciplines,” Crampton believes these type of experiences in the classroom supporting the growth of the students as humans and honors their abilities.

Crampton plans to use her award to disseminate ideas and learn from her peers through conference travel and potentially introduce students to technology and creative music composition through the garageband app.
