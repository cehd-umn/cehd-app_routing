About the OML

The Observational Methods Lab (OML) provides technical assistance and support as needed to ongoing projects and new research initiatives that rely on direct observational data,  and computer-assisted real time data.

The OML is equipped with two videotape coding stations, one G4 Mac digital image processing station, two dedicated Pentium for data downloading and sequential analysis, as well as digital cameras, timers, and other related equipment to assist with observational data collection or student training.  Equipment and material are available on an as-needed or reserved basis. Please contact the OML at oml@umn.edu  for further information on services and training.

The overall goal of the OML is to provide a context in which faculty and graduate students develop observational codes that assess the social behavior and interactions of parents, children, and teachers within a variety of different contexts, including home, preschool, classroom, playground or other settings.  Behaviors within each of these contexts are thought to be relevant to the development, maintenance, and/or treatment of child antisocial behavior patterns as well as understanding the development of pro-social behavior.  Overall, observation data collection and analysis procedures are playing an increasingly important role in our assessment of outcomes due to both intervention and prevention programs. 

The OML is consistent with the technology mission of the College of Education and Human Development, and, as such, the OML is a Department of Educational Psychology core resource. 

The OML is located in the Education Sciences Building, rooms 146-150. To see the lab or to schedule a time for checking out resources email oml@umn.edu.

The OML has a variety of resources to assist in data collection.
