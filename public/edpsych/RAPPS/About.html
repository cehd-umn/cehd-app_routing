About the Project

Project Ratio and Proportion Problem Solving (Project RAPPS)

Project RAPPS Overview

Our study targets students’ learning of ratio and proportion, which are critically important concepts in middle school mathematics instruction and topics that students have great difficulty with. We are particularly interested in helping students solve word problems using ratio and proportion. Although students may have learned to find answers when solving problems in these topic areas, there is evidence from state, national, and international assessments that many students do not understand the procedures that they use and cannot monitor their problem solving, both of which can lead to difficulties in solving novel problems.

Our instructional approach, schema-based instruction (SBI), focuses students’ attention on the essential role of the mathematical structure of problems. We highlight problem structure through the use of schemas, which are essentially diagrams that help the learner categorize various problem types and focus on critical problem features to determine the most appropriate strategies for solving a problem. Self-monitoring involves teaching students to use a problem solving heuristic or plan that provides guidance and structure for approaching unfamiliar problems.

Project RAPPS Intervention

Below is a description of Project RAPPS instructional approach that we are exploring in our research.

  1. Problem-based mathematics: Instruction that uses word problems for both learning and practicing skills. Word problems help students make connections between real world contexts and the mathematical operations they are learning in school.
  2. Direct instruction: An alternative to the student-directed/discovery approach that is characterized by explicit instruction and active student participation.
  3. Schema-based instruction: The following features characterize our instructional approach. Specifically, our instructional model
    • uses schema training to help students see the underlying mathematical structure of word problems through the use of schematic diagrams.
    • focuses on multiple solution strategies.
    • emphasizes metacognitive strategy knowledge by having students monitor their cognitive thought processes (i.e., problem solving behavior) to focus on comprehending the problem, representing the problem, planning to solve the problem using appropriate strategies, and reflecting on the solution via the use of “think-alouds.” 

Project RAPPS Timeline

YEAR ONE:

  • Developed an instructional program to promote 7th grade students’ understanding of ratio and proportion word problems using SBI.
  • Created and tested lessons and measures in four 7th grade classrooms in Pennsylvania.
  • Investigated how students of different achievement levels (i.e., high, average, low) responded to the intervention.

YEAR TWO:

  • Tested the curriculum developed during Year 1, with a larger sample of 7th grade students in Minnesota.
  • Evaluated the effects of adding small group tutoring to SBI.

YEAR THREE:

  • Refined the curriculum based on Year 2 study and tested it with a different cohort of 7th grade students in 3 school districts in Minnesota.

YEAR FOUR:

  • Year 4 seeks to investigate how SBI moderates student level characteristics associated with math
    (e.g, math computation, conceptual and procedural knowledge of fractions) and reading to predict end-of-intervention performance on math problem solving.
  • SBI will be implemented in secenteen 7th grade classrooms in 2 school districts.
