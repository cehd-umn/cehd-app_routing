Advanced Technological Education (ATE)

The aim of the Advanced Technological Education (ATE) program is to increase the number and quality of technicians in the nation.  The ATE program was established in 1992 and at that time was the only Congressionally-mandated program at the National Science Foundation (NSF).  The Scientific and Advanced Technology Act of 1992 led to the creation of the ATE program, “to establish a national advanced technician program, utilizing the resources of the National’s two-year associated-degree-granting colleges, to expand the pool of skilled technicians in strategic advanced technology fields, to increase the productivity of the Nation’s industries, and to improve the competitiveness of the United States in international trade, and for other purposes” (Public Law 102-476).

The Division of Undergraduate Education (DUE) and the Division of Elementary, Secondary and Informal Education (ESIE) at NSF jointly administer the ATE program.  NSF funds projects and centers across the nation to carry out the ATE program’s mission.  Currently the ATE program supports 10 Centers of Excellence and approximately 150 active projects.  Centers focus on systemic approaches to technician education, usually within a specific discipline, and are expected to have broad impact.  Projects focus on specific aspects of technician education, such as standards development, curriculum development, and faculty development.  All centers and most projects create extensive partnerships with businesses and industry, other two-year colleges, four-year colleges, and universities and secondary schools.  The focus of ATE is community colleges.  Activities include collaboration, materials development, program improvement, and professional development.

More information about the ATE Program can be found at http://www.nsf.gov/funding/pgm_summ.jsp?pims_id=5464.

 

Evaluation Information

Evaluator:  Arlen Gullickson, The Evaluation Center at Western Michigan University

  • The ATE evaluation has consisted of a Web-based survey of all the projects and a series of site visits to 13 project sites.
  •  Evaluation is external and guided by two advisory groups with expertise and experience in the ATE program.  Advisory group roles included assistance to construct evaluation instruments and assistance to conduct site visits.  The evaluation team developed site visit protocols.
  • Project members’ participation included review of the Web-based survey questions, which were then revised based on their input.
  • Site visit reports were shared with individual sites, but not with the National Science Foundation (NSF).  Decisions have been participant-oriented, as the evaluation team provides information to the projects and is receptive to their feedback.

More information about the ATE evaluation can be found on The Evaluation Center's website http://www.wmich.edu/evalctr/ate/ate.html.
