About the Project

Project MARS is the culmination of three years of previous research, which originated with a study conducted in one middle school in Pennsylvania. In 2007, 148 seventh grade students participated in two 2-week intervention studies, which focused on solving ratio, proportion and percent word problems using schema-based instruction (SBI).  In 2008, the study relocated with the Principle Investigator (Dr. Asha Jitendra) to the University of Minnesota.  The effectiveness of the SBI intervention was investigated with a sample of 475 students recruited from three middle schools in two suburban districts in Minnesota. The SBI program was revised to meet the MN mathematics standards, and the time allocated to the topics of ratio, proportion, and percent was increased to six weeks. During the 2009 -2010 academic year, we conducted the third study that included 1,163 seventh-grade students from six schools across three districts in MN. In sum, the fully developed six-week SBI program is based on a strong theoretical foundation, with empirical support from three experimental studies, which to date include over 1,800 seventh-grade students.

The Project MARS study is intended to extend the previous research on this topic and assess students’ learning of ratio and proportion, critically important concepts in middle school mathematics, where a majority of U.S. students continue to struggle and under perform. There is evidence from state, national, and international assessments that many students do not understand the procedures they use and cannot monitor their problem solving.

Our instructional approach, schema-based instruction (SBI), incorporates four features: priming the mathematical structure of problems, visual representations, procedural flexibility, and metacognitive strategy knowledge. SBI focuses students’ attention on the essential role of the mathematical structure of problems. We highlight problem structure through the use of schematic diagrams that help the learner categorize various problem types and represent the quantitative relationships described in the problem and to determine the most appropriate strategies for solving a problem. Students are taught to use a problem solving heuristic that provides guidance and structure for approaching unfamiliar problems and is used to help them monitor and reflect on the problem solving process. Our SBI curriculum, which includes two curricula units (ratio/proportion and percent), is not only aligned with the MN Mathematics Standards, but also the Common Core State Standards in mathematics.
