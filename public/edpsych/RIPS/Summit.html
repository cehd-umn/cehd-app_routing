“Back to the Future” of Special Education:
Individualizing Education through Data-Based Programming

Thursday, September 29, 2011
8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

Friday, September 30, 2011
8:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.

This invitational Summit on School-Based Problem Solving will begin with a lecture by Douglas Fuchs (Vanderbilt University). Lynn Fuchs (Vanderbilt University) will deliver a second address in the afternoon. Both lectures are followed by breakout discussions on related themes and directions for future research. University of Minnesota Emeritus Professor Stan Deno will reflect upon and wrap up the day’s deliberations. A second day is planned to shape a national research agenda on topics related to school-based problem solving. Discussion leaders and participants include scholars from institutions across the nation (see agenda). Ted Christ and Kristen McMaster, Co-Directors of the University of Minnesota Research Institute for Problem-Solving (RIPS), are conference hosts.

The purpose of the summit is to generate a research agenda for the evaluation, development and dissemination of data-based decision-making and experimental teaching methods.

Community partners are invited to the MITER lecture: Doug Fuchs’ morning address, from 8:30 to 10:00 a.m. University of Minnesota Special Education and School Psychology faculty members are encouraged to stay to participate in summit workgroup discussions. Special Education, School Psychology and MITER graduate students are invited to remain for the full conference as observers and recorders. Refreshments and box lunches will be served to those with reservations.

Smart RTI: A Next-Generation Approach to Multi-Level Prevention

Douglas Fuchs, Ph.D., is Nicholas Hobbs Professor of Special Education and Human Development at Vanderbilt University, and Senior Investigator in the Vanderbilt-Kennedy Center.

“Smart RTI” is a research-based approach that’s meant to help practitioners make efficient use of school resources while maximizing their students’ opportunities to achieve success. The presenter will discuss the research for (a) multi-stage screening to identify risk for academic difficulty; (b) multi-stage assessment to determine the level of instructional intensity required; and (c) special education that supports preventive efforts for most difficult-to-teach children.

Dr. Doug Fuchs has conducted programmatic research on response-to-intervention as a method for identifying children with learning disabilities and on instructional methods for improving reading and math outcomes for children with and without disabilities. He and his colleagues have published more than 300 empirical studies in peer-review journals on these and other topics. In 2010, he was described by Forbes Magazine as one of 14 “revolutionary educators.” In 2008, he was identified by Thompson ISI as one of 350 "most highly cited" researchers in the social sciences. In 2006, he was named among “100 Distinguished Alumni” in the first 100 years of the College of Education and Human Development, University of Minnesota.

Enhancing Data Use, Instructional Planning and Student Learning within Data-Based Programming: Some Lessons Learned

Lynn Fuchs, Ph.D. is the Nicholas Hobbs Professor of Special Education and Human Development at Vanderbilt University.

This session begins with an overview of what curriculum-based measurement (CBM) is and a description of the original framework within which CBM research was conceptualized. Then the presenter focuses on a program of research designed to promote understanding of how CBM data can be used productively for improving instructional planning and student learning. The session concludes with a proposal for future, related research.

Dr. Lynn Fuchs conducts research on assessment methods for enhancing instructional planning and on instructional methods for improving reading and math outcomes for students at risk for and with learning disabilities. She has published more than 300 empirical studies in peer-review journals and sits on the editorial boards of a variety of journals including the Journal of Educational Psychology, Scientific Studies of Reading, Reading Research Quarterly, Elementary School Journal, Journal of Learning Disabilities, and Exceptional Children. She has been identified by Thompson ISI as one of 350 "most highly cited" researchers in the social sciences and has received a variety of awards to acknowledge her research accomplishments for enhancing reading and math outcomes for children with and without disabilities.
