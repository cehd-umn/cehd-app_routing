Complex Adaptive Systems as a Model for Network Evaluations (CASNET)

CASNET is a three-year, full-scale project that will study complexity theory as a framework for fostering evaluation capacity building (ECB) within a network. This study will use a combined design-based research/case study approach to study and iteratively refine the evaluation system of a single case, the Nanoscale Informal Science Education Network (NISE Net, DRL 0532536, 0940143), the design of which has been informed by complexity theory.

Intellectual Merit

CASNET will yield new insights on (1) the implications of complexity theory for designing evaluation systems that promote widespread and systemic use of evaluation within a network, and (2) complex system conditions that foster or impede ECB within a network. While only one network will be studied, the emphasis on the underlying theoretical frameworks, complexity theory and ECB, extends the application of the findings to a range of network types. The design of the NISE Net evaluation system has been informed by these frameworks, thus affording the opportunity for simultaneous refinement of both the theories and the evaluation system.

This study will use a case study research method to generate rich descriptions, combined with design-based research (DBR) to enable iterative changes to both the guiding theories and the evaluation system design over time. Both case study and DBR are well-suited for this study as each embraces complexity by not isolating variables and examining the whole system. Consistent with DBR, this three-year study features an iterative design with distinct phases. Between phases, the initial framework for the conditions of complex systems that foster or impede ECB within a network will be redefined, and that redefinition will inform substantial changes to the NISE Net evaluation system. Each phase uses case studies as a means for collecting and/or analyzing data. Within the larger case of NISE Net, multiple groups will be studied as smaller cases, thus affording the research team the ability to explore how the conditions that foster or impede ECB within NISE Net may vary based on the multiple conditions present. Such variations may yield important insights for other networks.

CASNET features an integrated research team that will be led by researchers from University of Minnesota, who will work in close collaboration with the NISE Net evaluation team. Such an integrated team enables a design-based research approach where iterative changes are made to the design of the evaluation system based emergent findings, while simultaneously affording opportunities for outside interpretations of the findings for the theories.

Broader Impacts

In today’s globally-oriented and interconnected world, networks have gained increased prominence. Despite their growing presence, little is known about network evaluations. Networks are complex adaptive systems, characterized by non-linearity and decentralized decision making. Given this, traditional evaluation approaches that rely on linear evaluation methods and a centralized evaluator are not always appropriate. To date, the evaluation field has largely used complexity theory for descriptive purposes, with little discussion of its use for designing an evaluation system. It is this characteristic that makes CASNET unique.

By utilizing a design-based research approach, CASNET will generate research-based understandings of complexity theory and its implications for network evaluations, as well as a refined model for a complex evaluation system that fosters ECB within a network. Consistent with this dual focus, findings will be disseminated broadly through conference presentations, journal articles, and the project website, and the project will produce guides, tools, and models that can be used by other networks to design a complex, decentralized evaluation system.
